# The Institutional AI Readiness Pack
## *Self-Assessment and Implementation Tools for Responsible AI in Academic Research*

**The Institutional AI Readiness Pack is a university-wide assessment and implementation toolkit for responsible AI in research, spanning practice, people, policy, systems, procurement, data, disclosure, and oversight. It contains a twenty-cell institutional self-assessment, parallel surveys for doctoral students and for their advisors and supervisors, a model institutional policy, an AI-use disclosure standard, a supervisor–student agreement, role briefings for five levels of university leadership, and a worksheet that turns a score into named actions with named owners. Every instrument is licensed CC BY 4.0 so your institution can adapt it, translate it, and adopt it as its own.**

## What this is

This pack is the implementation companion to **[Responsible AI in Academic Research: A Competency Framework for Research Training](https://github.com/mzyphur/responsible-ai-in-research-training)** (Instats Policy Series, DOI [10.61700/t31oy23grr](https://doi.org/10.61700/t31oy23grr)).

That report set out what good looks like: a five-dimension competency framework and a four-level maturity grid for responsible AI use in research and research training. It also noted something the framework itself made unreachable — that no university in its sample of thirty-eight publishes its own scoring against the framework, so the top level of the fifth dimension was, at publication, *"a forward-looking standard that the framework's adopters can elect to populate."*

**This pack is how an institution populates it.** The report tells you what good looks like. The pack tells you where you actually stand, and what to do on Monday.

It is written for the people who can act on the answer: Vice-Presidents for Research, Deputy and Pro Vice-Chancellors, Deans and Associate Deans, Graduate School and Doctoral College leadership, research-integrity and graduate studies committees, and the bodies that represent graduate students.

## How to run the assessment

The design is **triangulated**, and that is the point. Leadership completes the institutional
self-assessment (A1). Doctoral students complete A2. Advisors and supervisors complete A3. A2 and A3 are written
as parallel forms so the answers can be compared item by item.

**The gap between what leadership believes and what graduate students report is the finding.** An
institution whose policy axis scores well and whose people axis does not has a policy nobody is
living by — and it will not discover that by asking itself.

Scoring follows the report's own rules. Each of the twenty cells resolves to one of four levels. A
dimension is only as mature as its weakest axis. The pack does not produce a single institutional
score, because the report is explicit that *"the diagnostic value of the grid is
the dimensional pattern, not a single score."*
